Understanding Polygon.io Pricing in 2026: Plans, Costs & Limits

Polygon.io pricing is structured around several tiers. Note: As of October 30, 2025, Polygon.io rebranded to Massive.com — existing APIs, keys, and accounts remain functional. This guide references pre-rebrand pricing for historical comparison.

Polygon Pricing Tier 1: Starter Plan ($29/month equivalent)

The entry-level Polygon pricing tier starts around $29/month (per historical data from polygon.io/pricing, pre-rebrand). This plan includes access to US equity data (stocks and ETFs), with unlimited API calls but limited WebSocket connections and data features. Historical data is limited to the most recent 5 years. For AI Agent developers, the Starter tier lacks WebSocket depth needed for real-time streaming workloads.

Polygon Pricing Tier 2: Developer Plan ($99/month equivalent)

The Developer tier at $99/month (polygon.io/pricing, pre-rebrand) expands access to options data, forex, and crypto. Unlimited API calls with higher WebSocket connection limits. Historical data extends to 10+ years. However, the subscription model still follows fixed monthly tiers — if your AI Agent needs exceed the plan's connection limits during peak usage, you are forced to upgrade to the next tier.

Polygon Pricing Tier 3: Advanced Plan ($199/month equivalent)

For mid-tier needs, the Advanced plan at $199/month provides higher WebSocket connection limits and extended historical data. Many AI Agent teams find that this tier is necessary simply to handle realistic production workloads, making the true cost higher than the entry-level price suggests.

Polygon Pricing Tier 4: All-Access Plan ($399/month equivalent)

The top published tier is All-Access at $399/month with maximum WebSocket connections and full data coverage. For high-volume AI Agent applications, this is the minimum viable tier. Above this, Enterprise plans with custom contracts are also available (pricing not publicly published). For AI Agent teams evaluating alternatives, these recurring monthly costs are a significant consideration — especially when comparing against pay-per-use models that have no fixed monthly commitment.
